Part 3 — the energy the cycle makes

The Krebs cycle: where the energy gets caught

Same loop, same carbon counts as before — now showing where the energy gets caught. Three kinds of payout: big batteries, a small battery, and one cash coin. The carbons leaving as CO2 are just exhaust; the batteries are the real prize.

Per lap

3 big batteries 1 small battery 1 cash coin 2 CO2

Per glucose (2 laps)

6 big batteries 2 small batteries 2 cash coins 4 CO2

What happens to the batteries next

The Krebs loopcharges the batteries, pays out the coin
→
Charged batteriescarried to the cell's power plant
→
ATPthe energy your cells actually spend

At the power plant, each big battery is cashed in for about 2.5 ATP, each small battery for about 1.5 ATP, and the cash coin is already worth 1 ATP. That adds up to roughly 10 ATP per lap — about 20 per glucose from this stage alone. The coin is the only energy paid out directly; nearly all of the payoff comes from cashing in the batteries.

The 2.5 and 1.5 figures are widely used textbook estimates; exact values vary slightly between sources. Battery names, if you want them: big battery = NADH, small battery = FADH2, cash coin = GTP (readily converted to ATP).
