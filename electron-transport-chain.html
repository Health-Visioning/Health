Why ATP matters

All that machinery exists to make one molecule. ATP (adenosine triphosphate) is the cell's energy currency: a small, rechargeable package of energy that every part of the cell knows how to spend.

ATP ⇄ ADP + Pi + energy

Breaking off the last phosphate releases a burst of usable energy. The waterfall you just watched puts that phosphate back, so the same molecules are spent and recharged over and over.

It powers movement

Muscle fibers use ATP to slide against each other and contract. Without it, muscles lock stiff, which is what happens in rigor mortis.

It runs the cell's pumps

The Na⁺/K⁺ pump uses ATP to keep nerve cells ready to fire. Your brain spends a large share of your energy budget on this alone.

It builds things

Making proteins, DNA, and membranes are uphill reactions. ATP pays for them by coupling to each step.

It's recycled constantly

The body holds only a few dozen grams at once but turns over roughly its own weight in ATP each day, so production has to keep pace with use.

That is why the chain is so vulnerable. Cyanide blocks Complex IV, so electrons can't reach oxygen. The waterfall backs up, the tank drains, and ATP production collapses within minutes. Try dragging the slider to zero to see the same thing happen here.

The payoff is large: one glucose molecule yields about 30 ATP in total, and the electron transport chain accounts for most of them.
