How insulin works, simplified

Think of insulin as a key. Your cells are locked doors, and sugar is the fuel waiting outside.

Sugar (fuel) Insulin (key) Door on a cell

From high sugar in the blood

  • Eyes: blurry vision
  • Kidneys: filters wear out
  • Nerves: numb or tingling feet
  • Heart: higher risk of heart attack and stroke

From high insulin

  • Weight: more fat stored around the belly
  • Blood pressure: salt and water build up
  • Hunger: cravings and energy crashes
  • Liver: fat builds up

Type 1 diabetes

The pancreas can’t make the keys. Doctors replace them with insulin injections or a pump.

Type 2 diabetes

Keys exist, but the locks have gotten stiff. The pancreas makes more keys, and eventually it can’t keep up.

What goes wrong when it keeps happening

When insulin resistance lasts for years, both extra sugar and extra insulin in the blood take a toll.

Too much sugar in the blood

  • Eyes: tiny blood vessels get damaged, which can blur or cloud vision.
  • Kidneys: the filters get overworked and can slowly fail.
  • Nerves: tingling or numbness, often starting in the feet.
  • Heart and arteries: higher risk of heart attack and stroke.
  • Healing: cuts heal slowly and infections are more likely.

Too much insulin

  • Weight gain: insulin tells the body to store fat, especially around the belly.
  • Hunger and crashes: sugar swings bring cravings and tiredness.
  • Blood pressure: the body can hold on to extra salt and water, pushing pressure up.
  • Fatty liver: more fat builds up in the liver and in the blood.
  • Skin: dark, velvety patches on the neck or armpits are a common sign.

These effects build up slowly, and early on many people feel fine, which is why regular blood tests matter. This page is general information, not medical advice.
