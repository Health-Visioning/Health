Disease-specific codes: how far did 2020–2024 move from its projection?

Each card charts a code's age-standardized rate and reports the same analysis on its death counts underneath. The blue line is the projection chosen from log-linear, exponentially weighted regression and rolling regression, whichever matches the pre-2020 data best; the blue band is that method's typical 1–5-year forecast error from backtesting. Readings are also expressed as standard-deviation equivalents, the z-score a reading would carry judged against how often quiet pre-2020 years produce it. How this works, and how far to trust it
  • Methods. Log-linear (a constant percentage change per year) competes with exponentially weighted regression and rolling regression; EMA and straight-line fits are no longer used anywhere. The default comes from the size of the numbers (order 1, most 2000–2019 values under 10: the better of the two local techniques; orders 2 and 3: log-linear), and another technique replaces it only when its backtest error is at least 10% lower. Rates ended up with log-linear for 6, exponentially weighted regression for 106, rolling regression for 81 of 193 codes; the order rule was overridden on fit for 8. Counts ended up with log-linear for 111, exponentially weighted regression for 24, rolling regression for 58 of 193 codes; the order rule was overridden on fit for 82.
  • Marked steps. A marked step is a one-year jump, up or down, of at least 10% that is also an outlier against the series' normal year-to-year changes. It restarts the baseline at the step, but the baseline never has fewer than the 5 years closest to 2019. 34 codes have a step with enough data after it. For 6 codes (A41, F05, I63, R09, R64, X40-X49) the step is too recent, so the 5-year baseline straddles it; their projections are unreliable, and their cards carry a Short baseline tag. Projections are frozen at 2019 and never see 2020 data.
  • Typical error and gap ÷ error. Typical error is the root-mean-square relative error of the method's 1–5-year-ahead forecasts in backtests on pre-2020 data. Gap is the 2020–24 average against the projection's average, and gap ÷ error counts the gap in typical-error units. A gap inside one band is an ordinary forecast miss.
  • Standard-deviation equivalents. Gap ÷ error is not a z-score: in quiet pre-pandemic years the median code already reaches about 1 error band, so the bands are too narrow. Each reading is therefore converted to the normal z-score with the same chance of being exceeded as it was in quiet years (2014–18 and 2015–19). The tiers come out at roughly: watch (1.5 to 2 bands) ≈ 1.1 std dev, weak (2 to 3) ≈ 1.3, moderate (3+) ≈ 1.7, and strong (4+ in both measures) ≈ 2.0 in each measure. Quiet years reached those levels about 3 in 10, about 1 in 5, about 1 in 10 and about 1 in 20 respectively, so the watch and weak tiers are barely distinguishable from chance. Outside 2 bands, 2020–24 had 13% of counts and 12% of rates, against 18–19% in quiet years. Only the strong and moderate tiers deserve attention. A + after a figure means it is beyond every quiet-year case.
  • Pattern. Compares the five yearly gaps (2020–24) with the projection: within typical error in every year; mixed (above in some years and below in others); or above/below with the gap widest in 2024, steady, peaked earlier and partly closed (under three-quarters of its peak by 2024), or back within typical error by 2024. Counts get their own pattern line.
  • Counts and rates. The two measures agreed on flag or no flag for 182 of 193 codes, and all 19 of the codes flagged in both pointed the same way. Counts gaps can include population growth and ageing; across codes the median gap is similar (+0.1% for counts, -0.2% for rates), but individual codes differ.
  • Driver families are hypotheses. They come from each code's ICD-10 range and direction of change, not from a test of cause. Family 8, "Not independently classified," holds codes that no driver rule covers (the assault codes X95 and X99, and Down's syndrome Q90) and four codes (L08, L89, Y86, J45) whose family would otherwise rest on their ICD-10 letter alone. R99 is a certification catch-all, not a disease. X40-X49 (accidental poisoning, which holds the opioid overdose deaths) is a block rather than a single code, but is included at your request.
  • COVID-19 displacement flag. A code is flagged when it ran below its projection by 2+ error bands in either measure and at least half of its 2015–19 deaths were at age 80 or over. "Persists" means the 2023–24 deficit is at least four-fifths of the 2020–22 deficit. A deficit that persists as COVID-19 deaths fell fits earlier deaths depleting the at-risk group better than same-year substitution, but fewer diagnoses during the pandemic would look the same, and these tables cannot separate them.
