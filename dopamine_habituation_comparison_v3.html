Caveats — read before you cite this anywhere

Both the curves and the dependence scores are illustrative, not measured data. They're built from general knowledge of relative addictive potential, not from a single study or dataset — don't attach a citation to the specific numbers.

Real tolerance doesn't follow a clean exponential curve. It varies by dose, frequency, route of administration (smoked/injected substances hit faster and harder than oral ones), genetics, and individual brain chemistry.

"Dependence strength" collapses several different things researchers usually separate: physical dependence, psychological craving, withdrawal severity, and likelihood of compulsive use. A single score is a simplification of all four.

Ranking addictive potential is genuinely contested. Studies like Nutt et al. (2007, 2010) rank substances across multiple harm/dependence dimensions and don't always agree on ordering — this is a common approximation, not a consensus finding.

Food and behavioral "addictions" are debated as a category — whether ordinary food reward compares directly to drug dependence is itself an active scientific discussion.

This is for building intuition, not clinical or dosing decisions. If you or someone you know is dealing with substance use, a doctor or addiction specialist can give guidance this chart isn't designed to provide.
