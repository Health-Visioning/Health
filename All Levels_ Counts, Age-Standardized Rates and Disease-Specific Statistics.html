Column key, filters and how the standard-deviation equivalent is calculated
  • What is in the table. Chapter II is restricted to malignant neoplasms (C00-C97). Sub-levels and codes with fewer than 100 deaths in 2022 are omitted (chapters are always kept), and the provisional COVID-19 codes (Chapter XXII, U00-U49 and U07) are removed at every level. X40-X49 (accidental poisoning, which holds the opioid overdose deaths) is a block rather than a single code, but is treated as disease-specific. B99 appears twice in the source with identical figures; the statistics are on the first row.
  • Views. Hierarchy keeps the chapter → sub-level → code structure; clicking a row with a chevron opens it, and any filter or search shows matching rows with their parents. Disease-specific only lists the codes flat and is sortable: click a column header to sort (clicking one from the hierarchy view switches to this view).
  • Gap is the 2020–24 average against the projection's average for those years. Typical error is the root-mean-square relative error of that method's 1–5-year-ahead forecasts in backtests on pre-2020 data. Gap ÷ error is the gap in typical-error units (positive: above the projection; negative: below).
  • Std dev equiv. converts gap ÷ error into the z-score with the same chance of being exceeded as it was in quiet years. I took every code-measure pair from two pre-pandemic placebo periods (2014–18 and 2015–19), found the share with a gap ÷ error at least as large as the row's, and looked up the normal z-score with that two-sided chance. A + after the figure means the row is beyond every quiet-year case, so it is capped at about ±3.2. It is a rough, empirical equivalent, not a formal test.
  • Strength tiers use the larger gap ÷ error of the two measures: Watch 1.5 to 2 (≈ 1.0 std dev), Weak 2 to 3 (≈ 1.3), Moderate 3+ (≈ 1.7), Strong 4+ in both measures (≈ 2.0); under 1.5 is within normal variation. Quiet years reach the Watch and Weak levels often, so only Moderate and Strong deserve attention.
  • Order is set by digits: 1 means most 2000–2019 values are under 10, 2 means 10 to 99, 3 means 100 or more. Method is chosen from log-linear, EMA, exponentially weighted regression and rolling regression; an asterisk means it replaced the order-rule default because its backtest error was at least 10% lower. Cannot model means a major step left fewer than 5 years of data after it.
