Which causes are still off trend? Trend charts by pattern and driver

Each card charts one individual ICD-10 code's age-standardized rate per 100,000 (standardized to the 2019 population) against its pre-pandemic trend. Use the menus to filter by 2020–24 pattern and by driver family. Cards are ordered by the strength of their z-score. Chapter rows, sub-group ranges and the provisional COVID-19 codes (U00-U49) are not included. The one exception is X40-X49 (accidental poisoning, which includes opioid overdoses): a block rather than a single code, added back at your request. A purple tag marks codes flagged for possible COVID-19 displacement (see the notes).

How to read this, and how far to trust it
  • Only individual codes are counted. Nothing is counted at more than one level, but neighbouring codes can still reflect the same process, for example gastric and duodenal ulcers.
  • 49 of the 51 still-above-trend codes are also red (z-score >=3) on age-standardized rates. The other 2 are red (z-score >=3) on raw counts only, so population growth and ageing may explain part of them. Red here means a z-score of 3 or more in absolute value, above +3 or below -3.
  • The driver families are hypotheses, not tested causes. They come from each code's ICD-10 range and direction of change. Family 1 is weakest for its 5 still-above-trend musculoskeletal codes (arthrosis, spine disorders, osteoporosis): those conditions rarely cause death on their own, so a change in how deaths are certified is at least as likely as deferred care.
  • "Back within normal variation" is a lenient test. It means 2024 sits within two standard deviations of the pre-2020 scatter around the trend line. 13 of the 33 codes in that group sit between 1.5 and 2 standard deviations, close to the cut-off.
  • X40-X49 is an exception to the single-code rule. It is a block (accidental poisoning, which includes opioid overdoses), included at your request. It is red (z-score >=3) on both counts and age-standardized rates, but it is classed "back within normal variation" only narrowly: 2024 is 1.9 standard deviations above trend (16.6 against 13.1 per 100,000), having fallen from its 2021 peak of 20.2. Alcoholic liver disease (K70) and alcohol-related mental disorders (F10) are the other two codes in family 3, and both are also back near trend.
  • Possible COVID-19 displacement is flagged on 13 below-trend codes. The idea is that COVID-19 killed frail people earlier than they would otherwise have died of old-age diseases, leaving fewer deaths for those causes. A code is flagged when it ran below trend and at least half of its 2015–19 deaths were at age 80 or over. Timing is the useful test: COVID-19 deaths fell from 20,185 in 2022 to 5,060 in 2024, yet 9 of the flagged codes, including dementia (F03), Alzheimer's disease (G30), atrial fibrillation (I48) and aortic valve disorders (I35), stayed at least four-fifths as far below trend in 2023–24 as in 2020–22 (Alzheimer's: 16% below trend in 2022, 19% in 2024). Simple same-year substitution of COVID-19 for the recorded cause would predict those gaps closing, so persistence fits earlier deaths depleting the at-risk group better. Fewer dementia diagnoses during the pandemic, or a pre-existing decline steeper than a straight line, would look the same, and these tables cannot separate the explanations. The 4 respiratory codes whose gaps have narrowed (J18, J44, J47, J98) also fit reduced infection transmission.
  • Family 8, "Not independently classified," holds four codes whose family would otherwise rest on their ICD-10 letter alone: L08 (other local skin infections), L89 (pressure ulcers), Y86 (sequelae of other accidents) and J45 (asthma). All four are above trend. They are shown separately so they are not given the same confidence as the infection and complication codes left in family 4.
  • R99 is kept, but it is not a disease. It is a certification catch-all and the only code in family 7.
  • Family 5 contains none of the above-trend codes by design. It explains the codes that ran below trend (respiratory disease, suicide, perinatal conditions, viral hepatitis).
