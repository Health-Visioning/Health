One page, start to finish

From food to the Krebs cycle, carbon by carbon

Follow the carbons the whole way: food is broken into glucose, glucose is cut into 3-carbon pyruvates, each pyruvate is trimmed to a 2-carbon acetyl piece — and that piece joins a 4-carbon piece to start the cycle.

Step 1
🍽️ Food

Carbs, protein, and fat — each stores energy in its chemical bonds, but none of it is usable yet.

Step 2
🦷 Digestion

Mouth, stomach, and small intestine break each food type into its simplest building block: carbs → glucose (6 carbons).

Fat breaks into fatty acids, protein breaks into amino acids — different starting routes, but they all end up feeding into the same doorway later. This page follows the glucose route, since it's the clearest one.
Step 3
🩸 Bloodstream

Glucose is absorbed and carried through the blood to cells all over the body.

Step 4 · before the mitochondria
✂️ Glucose chopped in half

In the cell's main fluid, glucose (6 carbons) is split into two pyruvates (3 carbons each). No oxygen needed for this part.

6 carbons, cut clean in half → two identical 3-carbon pyruvates. One glucose always makes exactly two.

Step 5
🚪 Pyruvate enters the mitochondria

Pyruvate crosses into the mitochondria — the cell's power plant, and the only place the Krebs cycle can run.

Step 6 · just inside the door
💨 One more carbon trimmed

Each pyruvate (3 carbons) loses one carbon as CO2 and becomes a 2-carbon acetyl piece, carried by CoA (Acetyl-CoA).

One carbon clipped off as CO2; the remaining 2-carbon piece clips onto CoA — a handle, not a carbon source.

The hand-off point
🎟️ The 2-carbon acetyl piece is the ticket into the Krebs cycle

It's about to join a 4-carbon piece that's already waiting inside the mitochondria.

Step 7 · inside the mitochondria
🔄 The Krebs cycle: count the carbons

Follow one molecule around the loop: it gains 2, loses 1, loses 1 — and ends up exactly where it started.

Tally per original glucose molecule: 6 carbons → 2 pyruvates (3 each) → 2 acetyl pieces (2 each) + 2 CO2 already exhaled → the Krebs loop above runs twice, once per acetyl piece, each lap losing 2 more carbons as CO2. Along the way the cycle also catches small bursts of energy in rechargeable batteries — that part is a separate diagram.
